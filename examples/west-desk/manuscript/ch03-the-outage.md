# 19 June 2001

## Ten past eleven

The tie went out at ten past eleven at night. I know the minute because
Priya wrote it in her note to Marcus, and because the operator's posting
said the same thing in the morning, and because for the next eighteen
months every settlement dispute the desk had came back to that minute.

A forced outage, the posting said. Derate to forty percent through at
least Thursday. Priya called Marcus at home. Marcus, by his own account,
called nobody, went back to sleep, and in the morning walked to the desk
and said that nobody schedules across the tie until the operator lifts the
derate. Tomas held the noon submission. It was the first time I had seen
him hold anything, and I wrote that down that night in a journal I had
started keeping, because it seemed like the kind of thing that would
matter later. It did.

## Forty, then sixty

The derate stepped. That was the thing nobody had planned for. Forty on
the Wednesday, and then on Thursday, if the second line held, sixty. Priya
explained it to Marcus in an email that answered his questions one at a
time between his own lines, the way she did when she wanted the record to
show exactly what had been asked and exactly what she had said. Is it
fixed at forty for the whole outage? No, it steps. And the noon
submission? Tomas is holding it.

We scheduled at forty on the forty days and at sixty on the sixty days,
and we did it under the eleven o'clock rule, which meant every schedule
was in the operator's hands an hour before it was needed, with the time on
it. I have said that the rule saved us. This is where it did.

## The letter

Somebody, and I think it was Marcus though the file says the template
belonged to nobody, wrote a letter to the operator at the end of the
month. We scheduled at forty percent on the days the derate was posted at
forty, it said, and we ask that settlement reflect it. It was two
sentences on a template that had been made years before, and the document
says it was created in 1998, which is a lie of the machine kind: the
template was created in 1998, the letter in June. Anyone reading the file
by its metadata alone would put the outage three years too early.

## What the statement said

The statement did not reflect it. Corinne caught it in November, when the
October settlement came in disagreeing with our scheduled volumes on the
tie for the outage week by something like eleven thousand megawatt hours,
all of it on the days the derate stepped. The operator had settled us at
sixty on the days we were told forty. She asked for the hourly detail and
told Marcus not to sign the statement before it came.

That is the whole dispute, and it took a year. But this chapter is about
June, and in June we did not know any of it. We knew the tie was out, the
derate stepped, the schedule was held, and the river was still high.
